The Meaning of Words - Word Association

The performing slide. It opens on ONE word, centred and close up: girl. Nothing else — no other dots, no headings, no grid. Resist filling the silence; let them look at it. The board moves under you. First press is the demonstration — girl → boy, you say both words yourself — and the pair slides LEFT as boy arrives. Second press brings sister in and the board slides UP to make room beneath. When the capitals arrive (press four) the camera pulls all the way back and the full board is on screen for the rest of the game. After that it holds still; the movement is only there to make the first three beats feel like one idea growing rather than a chart appearing. That single first arrow teaches the shape of the game without asking anything of the room. From then on every pair is TWO presses. Press once and the next word lands with its arrow already drawn, pointing at a dashed empty socket and a "?". Say the source word and stop talking — the arrow is doing the asking. When they shout it, press again and the "?" becomes the answer. sister → ? is the one that proves they've got it: the arrow is visibly the same one that took girl to boy, so the answer is already implied by the picture before anyone says "brother". Running order warms them up: gender (everyone shouts these), then capital countries → cities (still easy; after France→Paris say "and watch the next one…" so they feel Rome land the same way), then copper→Cu / zinc→Zn (now you're doing science), then the fun ones: Japan→sushi and Australia→Bunnings sausage. If it stalls on the symbols, prompt "what's on the periodic table?" Nineteen presses in all. Don't rush them — the ? beat is where the room does the work. Do NOT explain the vector yet — just play the game. The layout is deliberate: every arrow in a group is the same direction and the same length, and the four groups point four visibly different ways.
