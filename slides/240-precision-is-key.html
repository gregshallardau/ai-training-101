Precision Is Key

Words carry meaning — and the model never asks you to clarify. It takes every word you use, literally.

Confusable pairs — very different outcomes

Your word A confusable word The outcome
“fix” the bug “explain” the bug fix → the code changes. explain → nothing changes, it just talks about it.
“rewrite” the report “proofread” the report rewrite → a new version, style may change. proofread → your words stay, errors get fixed.
“draft” the email “send” the email draft → a copy for you to check. send → it leaves the building with your name on it.
“delete” that paragraph “hide” it delete → it's gone. hide → it's still there, just out of sight.

Good, better, best — sharper words, sharper outcome

Word choice Your words The outcome
good “Make it shorter.” Shorter — by how much? The model gets to guess.
better “Shorten it to fit in one email.” One email is a range, not a number — it can still drift.
best “Cut it to under 90 words.” A sharp number, no room to guess — ready to send as is.

And one output is another's input — your output is the next prompt, the next email, the next model's context. If your words are vague, the vagueness travels.

Bridge from the "words have meaning" section: precision is how that meaning gets across. Table 1 — these four pairs are words the audience actually types. Emphasise that the model never asks which member of the pair you meant; it picks the most likely one and commits to it. Table 2 — the good / better / best ladder shows the fix: words with sharp, defined meanings (a number, a boundary) leave nothing to guess, so the outcome is usable. Close on "one output is another's input": your words also seed everything that happens after you. Vague words don't die — they propagate.
