The prompt is tiny.
It does not contain your situation, your intent, or your constraints.
The model is not a mind reader.
It can predict language, but it cannot know what you meant to include.
Context is the missing input.
Better answers come from giving the model more of the world around the question.
Prompting is not asking magic words.
It is bringing the right context into the conversation.
This slide should feel like the vector slides: coloured objects, staged reveals, and a visual mechanism. The model has broad patterns, but the tiny prompt only sends “Write me an email.” The big red question mark is the point: the model has no idea which version of the task you mean. Then reveal the missing context cards. Who am I? Who is it for? What tone? What history? What relationship? What am I trying to achieve? The shift is: prompt engineering is less about magic phrasing and more about supplying the context that was invisible.
